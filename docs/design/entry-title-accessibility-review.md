# Entry title accessibility review

## Initial proposal review — 2026-09-20

Reviewed `entry-title-accessibility.md`, the detail layout in `apps/apple/JournalApp/Views/RootView.swift`, and `EntryRecoveryNotice.swift`. This is an independent preimplementation design/source review; no prototype or new execution evidence was supplied, and interactive Mac remains unavailable on the locked desktop.

**Revise before implementation.** A native, plain, vertically growing title field with the existing semantic font and no permanent new action is a good fit for the quiet editor. Keeping the complete value, autosave, standard selection/correction, existing margins and familiar “Title” placeholder is appropriate. Two visible lines can improve the known fixture without making the header dominate normal writing. The proposal needs two concrete decisions before its accessibility claim is reliable.

1. **P2 — Full-title access must also work when editing is disabled.** The current title uses `.disabled(!model.canEdit)`. The proposal relies on native selection/editing to scroll titles longer than two lines, yet retains that disabled behavior for recovered/conflicted entries. A disabled text field is not a reliable readable, selectable scrolling surface. Specify a read-only presentation that permits inspection of the entire title without enabling mutations: for example, native selectable, wrapping text within a bounded vertical scroll region, with the complete Title accessibility name/value. Keep its visual typography and placement consistent with the editable field. Verify actual long-title scrolling and selection in a deleted/unavailable entry; do not infer this from an editable-field test or a complete accessibility value alone.

2. **P2 — Allocate usable body space jointly, including all surrounding content.** A title cap of one third and recovery cap of one half of the same geometry leave only one sixth before the existing 40 points of header padding, conflict notice, unsupported-document explanation and save-failure controls. With the keyboard or a short window this can leave no readable body line. Conversely, one third of a keyboard-reduced viewport may be shorter than one title line at the largest text size. “Inspect for body starvation” is a verification request, not a defined layout response. Define the available height after fixed chrome and the layout fallback when one complete title line plus a usable body region and notices cannot fit. A concrete rule should preserve at least a full body line and a usable touch target, allow title/notice regions to scroll, and avoid clipping title glyphs. If those minima cannot fit together, use a conventional scrollable arrangement that lets users move the header/notices out of the way to reach the body, or another explicitly reviewed native fallback. Do not shrink the chosen text size or hide recovery/save-failure information. Include conflict and save-failure rows in the budget, not only the recovery notice.

Clarify the multiline field's Return behavior as part of the revision. Vertical TextField can permit inserted newlines; state whether Return inserts a title newline or moves focus to the body, and preserve any existing stored line breaks without silently normalizing content. This is a behavior decision, not a request for extra interface copy.

The revised verification plan should retain normal/largest dark-mode, more-than-two-line title, keyboard-visible beginning/end editing, full stored value after relaunch, body focus/formatting and complete accessibility name/value checks. Add the read-only long-title case and a combined notice/short-height case proving the fallback, with readable screenshots of both the title remainder and body. Empty title stays “Title”; loading, offline and save-failure behavior can remain the existing behavior provided the revised layout keeps their controls reachable. Source-level accessibility inspection is useful but must not be reported as VoiceOver verification.

No production source was changed. Re-review the revised layout/read-only behavior before implementing.

## Revision 2 review — 2026-09-20

**Approved for implementation, subject to actual native verification.** Reviewed the appended Revision 2 independently. It supersedes the original separate title/notice caps and closes both material design findings. One naturally sized header, bounded to half the keyboard-adjusted detail viewport, accounts jointly for recovery/conflict/unsupported/save-failure content and title padding. Moving save-failure controls into the same overflow region avoids consuming the body's remaining space. A single native outer scroll area can reveal full-height title lines and notices when the viewport is shorter than their content; no compressed font or duplicated focusable title field is required. Retain natural sizing for short headers rather than reserving half the editor unconditionally.

Selectable, wrapping Text with complete Title name/value closes the read-only access gap without accidentally enabling title edits. Keep the header's accessibility identity from combining or hiding its children; both its scrolling and native text selection must remain operable. The single stable expanding editable field is preferable to swapping field instances as geometry changes. Existing recovery background and margins preserve the visual hierarchy without a new visual container.

Native Next followed by the existing body-focus command is an appropriate deliberate transition into writing. Treat this as a required observed behavior: Return/Next must move focus to the body without inserting an unexpected newline, while pasted or already stored line breaks remain unchanged. A submit-label modifier alone is not execution evidence that a vertically expanding field submits as intended.

No remaining material preimplementation design blocker. Verify that the measured header has a stable nonzero initial layout, updates after entry/title/notice and keyboard changes, and does not move focus to a different entry. Actual normal/largest-text evidence must show title beginning/end and caret while typing, Next into a usable body, full read-only title scrolling/selection, and recovery plus save-failure controls reachable in the shared header. The half-height rule protects body allocation but does not prove a native field scrolls its caret correctly; the proposal's requirement to revise if that fails remains a release gate. No new permission or separate title-reading sheet is required. Mac and VoiceOver remain unverified, and this design approval is not source or rendered-UI acceptance.

## Native paste correction proposal review — 2026-09-21

**Approved for implementation; the demonstrated paste defect remains open until corrected execution evidence is inspected.** Read the appended proposal, hosted `TitleEditingTests.swift`, and failure lines in `/tmp/journal-title-paste.log`. The log shows the newline-only paste missing from both native and bound text and an unexpected submission count of 1. This is meaningful actual UIKit evidence, not a simulated delegate-call sequence. The current inspected test also includes undo assertions; they must be demonstrated by the corrected run, not inferred from the proposal.

Using UITextPasteDelegate's final insertion callback is a proportionate correction to the asynchronous paste timing problem. Apply the supplied loaded plain text to the supplied destination UITextRange through native replacement, with the paste guard scoped around that actual insertion. Do not reread the pasteboard, append at the current caret instead of the supplied range, or assign the entire title string to implement paste. Those shortcuts could target the wrong selection or disrupt native undo. Preserve exactly the loaded text, including single/newline-only and multiline content, and avoid an additional default insertion after the delegate performs it. Maintain the existing binding-change notification without duplicating the insertion or recording a second undo operation.

The existing per-entry control identity and guarded binding must remain in place so a late paste completion belonging to a removed title control cannot alter a newly selected draft. Keep keyboard Next behavior separate and verify it still submits once without inserting a newline after a paste operation. The design adds no UI or copy, so no new layout review is required solely for this correction.

For bounded acceptance, the hosted test should prove native and bound contents, no paste submission, and one-step native undo for multiline and newline-only paste. Add a selected-range replacement case, since the proposal explicitly takes responsibility for applying UIKit's supplied destination range; appending-only evidence cannot establish selection preservation. Marked-text input, cross-entry undo, VoiceOver and save-failure presentation remain separately unverified. No production source was changed in this review.

## Native paste source and hosted evidence closure — 2026-09-21

**The demonstrated newline-only paste defect is closed for the inspected native paste cases.** Inspected current `EntryTitleEditor.swift`, `TitleEditingTests.swift`, and the completed hosted-test portion of `/tmp/journal-title-paste-final.log`. The title view is its own UITextPasteDelegate. Its final insertion callback scopes `isPasting` with defer around native `replace(textRange, withText: attributedString.string)`, uses the supplied range, and returns the inserted range computed from the original insertion offset and UTF-16 length. The callback does not reread the pasteboard or append blindly; plain title text remains the intentional contract. The source uses the SDK's nonoptional UITextRange return signature. Native delegate binding propagation remains intact, with no extra manual second insertion or full-string paste assignment.

The hosted UIKit test calls real `paste(nil)` with multiline content, a newline alone, and a selected-range replacement. For each case it asserts exact native and bound text, zero submissions, native undo availability, and restoration of both native and bound contents with one undo. The inspected log reports this test passed in 0.094 seconds, zero failures. This is materially stronger evidence than a delegate-call surrogate; no reviewer rerun was performed. Appearance and the prior bounded visual approval are unchanged.

At inspection the same log had not yet recorded completion of the separately running EntryActions UI test, so no final combined-run success or new keyboard-Next evidence is claimed here. A direct programmatic `insertText` call is not treated as an actual keyboard action. Actual combined paste-then-keyboard-Next, marked-text composition, cross-entry undo, VoiceOver, save-failure layout and interactive Mac remain unverified.
