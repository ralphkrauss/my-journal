# Permanent deletion — independent design review

Reviewed 2026-09-20 against `permanent-deletion.md`, `screens.md` and the project requirements for native interaction, explicit irreversible scope, privacy and preservation of conflicting edits. This is a preimplementation design review; no deletion UI or executed purge behavior was inspected.

## Verdict

**Revise before exposing the frontend.** The basic direction is appropriate: separate destructive confirmation in Recently Deleted, exact captured scope, no automatic purge, shared-image preservation, explicit concurrent-edit handling, atomic commit and truthful limits on erasure. Three material details remain unresolved below. Bounded core preflight work can proceed independently, but it does not constitute approval to expose the action.

## Required revisions

1. **Make confirmation distinguish immediate local removal from eventual sync, and disclose backups.** The current sentence says removal occurs “on your devices after syncing”; an offline user could reasonably read that as meaning the entry remains locally recoverable until sync. Use entry copy: “This entry and its earlier versions will be removed from Journal on this device. This deletion will sync to your other devices when they connect. Copies may remain in archives, backups, and server history.” Follow with “You can’t undo this.” Use the corresponding journal/count wording, with the proposed zero/singular variants. If revoked devices cannot receive the marker, scope the second sentence to “your other connected devices” instead. The confirmation should never imply wiping previously downloaded agent/device copies. The proposal's internal Meaning and limits section is sound but does not substitute for accurate user-visible timing and retention copy.

2. **Specify deletion-conflict decisions and their irreversible consequences before implementation.** Keep Deletion / Keep Entry / Keep Entry as Copy are not yet sufficiently defined for a user-facing review. State whether Keep Deletion removes the newly arrived edited payload and its recoverable history, and require a separate destructive confirmation if it does; explain that exact loss beside the action. If the edited payload remains recoverable instead, state where it can be found and ensure that does not contradict permanent deletion. Specify whether Keep Entry revives the original identity, where it appears, and how a missing/deleted/conflicted parent is handled. Specify Keep Entry as Copy's destination selection and retained historical date/content/image behavior, using the existing explicit recovery destination flow when needed. The sheet must identify the deletion event and edited version with device/time, retain a full edited-content preview, provide Cancel, and revalidate the reviewed versions. No choice may implicitly win a changed conflict. These details matter because screens.md's ordinary conflict design keeps discarded originals recoverable; this special flow must explicitly describe any different preservation rule.

3. **Make journal scope and metadata-conflict recovery fully explicit.** State in the confirmation that the included count contains entries already in Recently Deleted; otherwise users may apply the previously established journal-restore distinction and assume independently deleted entries are excluded. Suggested sentence: “This includes entries already in Recently Deleted.” Show it when relevant. Clarify the user-facing result of Keep Journal after a journal deletion/settings conflict: preserving journal metadata does not recover purged children, so the choice must not imply that it restores the journal's prior entries. Define what the user sees when there are no remaining entries and how later unseen children reach existing Unavailable recovery. The current historical-only-record sentence also needs precision: does it mean existing exported archives retain those records, or that local archive export still includes them after deletion? Do not imply an archive is automatically created. State the actual boundary explicitly in the design and ensure it matches the confirmation's “earlier versions” claim.

## Accepted interaction and accessibility direction

A scrollable native confirmation, captured name/date/count, explicit destructive label and ordinary Cancel ordering fit the app. Vertical actions and full multiline labels at accessibility sizes are the correct reuse of the reviewed archive pattern. Keep name/date/count and scope copy readable and selectable where appropriate; destructive color must supplement the label. Initial focus must not automatically activate or privilege the destructive action, Escape/Cancel must dismiss before commit, and VoiceOver should encounter captured identity and consequences before actions.

Saving before preparation and revalidating all affected payloads/child membership at commit are appropriate. A save failure must retain the same draft with Export Entry and meaningful retry. Review Again must prepare a fresh snapshot and require a new explicit confirmation, never automatically resubmit. Review Changes must leave deletion uncommitted and require fresh preparation after conflict resolution. Lock must clear retained confirmation/conflict values, suppress late payload/error publication, and reconcile a durable commit before any stale draft flush. Unsupported content needs a usable active-sheet archive/export route, not just an update instruction.

The postcommit-refresh failure sentence should be refined to avoid suggesting a deleted entry needs to be displayed: “The entry was deleted, but Journal couldn’t update the view. Reopen Journal to continue.” Use the journal equivalent. It must present completion/close semantics, not a second destructive retry. For a missing or already-deleted target, close only that captured stale confirmation without mutating another selection.

## Surviving conflict-history clarification — 2026-09-20

**Approved before implementation.** The proposed distinction preserves unreviewed versions without weakening the deliberate destructive boundary: only a second-confirmed Keep Deletion purges surviving source history. Keep Entry retains surviving history under the revived original identity but does not reconstruct versions already purged. Keep Entry as Copy retains source history for lossless archive export, does not attach that history to the new copy, leaves the source marker in place, and keeps ordinary source-history restore unavailable. The earlier phrase “does not resurrect original history” must mean it does not reopen ordinary history recovery, not that it silently removes surviving history.

Use this exact explanation beside **Keep Entry as Copy**:

> Creates a new entry and keeps the original deleted. Any remaining earlier versions stay available in archive exports.

This adds one material retention sentence without introducing another confirmation or technical terms. It does not imply an archive is created automatically or claim that previously purged versions survive. Keep Entry can retain its reviewed explanation: “Keeps the edited entry. Earlier versions already deleted aren’t restored.” Preserve the usual accessible full-content review and explicit destination flow.

The destructive confirmation for Keep Deletion must bind the actual surviving history set as well as current/remote revisions. A newly retained superseded conflict arriving after confirmation capture changes destructive scope and requires fresh review; it must not be swept away under stale consent. The existing wording “this edited version and its earlier versions” remains appropriate only when that captured scope includes the surviving versions being purged. Keep Entry/Copy must preserve every additional surviving payload and referenced image, including superseded unreviewed remote conflicts, through the local transaction and lossless archive export. Source-marker visibility rules must not accidentally omit these retained histories from export or expose them through ordinary restore.

This is semantic/copy approval, not a source or execution disposition. Verify real-store history preservation versus explicit purge, changed-history confirmation rejection, copy retry identity, archive round-trip and source-marker restore blocking before UI exposure. It neither promises physical erasure nor changes the previously disclosed server/archive retention limits.
