# App Lock

App Lock asks for Face ID, Touch ID or Optic ID, or your device passcode or Mac login password, before My Journal shows your journals. Someone who picks up your unlocked phone or sits down at your Mac then can’t read them. It doesn’t change how your journals are encrypted.

App Lock is set separately on each device. It needs a passcode on iPhone and iPad, and a login password on the Mac.

## Turn on App Lock

Choose Settings > Privacy and turn on **Require Face ID**. The switch is called **Require Touch ID**, **Require Optic ID**, **Require Passcode** or **Require Login Password**, depending on the device. My Journal asks you to authenticate once to turn it on.

To turn App Lock off, turn the switch off. This asks you to authenticate too.

## When My Journal locks

- When it opens.
- On iPhone and iPad, as soon as you leave the app. While locked, the app switcher shows a cover instead of your journals.
- On the Mac, when the Mac’s screen locks, or when you choose My Journal > **Lock My Journal** (⌃⌘L).
- When you choose Settings > Privacy > **Lock My Journal**.

What you were writing is saved first. While My Journal is locked, agents can’t read your journals.

## Unlock

When My Journal opens, and when you return to it on iPhone or iPad, it asks for Face ID right away. Otherwise choose **Unlock with Face ID**, or the name of your device’s method. If Face ID doesn’t recognize you, use your passcode or login password instead.

If your device can’t authenticate you, choose **Use Master Password** (or your recovery key) on the lock screen. App Lock stays on.

## If your device no longer has a passcode

If you remove your device’s passcode, or your Mac login password, while App Lock is on, the next time you open My Journal it turns App Lock off and tells you. Set a passcode, then turn App Lock on again.

## What App Lock doesn’t do

- It isn’t encryption, and it doesn’t protect against malware or anyone who uses the same Mac user account.
- Anyone who knows your device passcode or login password can open My Journal.
- Keep your device protected too: a strong passcode or login password, and FileVault on the Mac.
